# Novelty scan: Verifiable Chain-of-Custody for Digital Evidence Files

*Updated: 2026-10-28 (first run 2026-10-08) | Method: targeted prior-art scan (not a systematic, scoping or other formal review) | This run added scholarly-index queries (OpenAlex, arXiv API) to the general web search | Confidence: Medium for "no forensic custody system uses witness cosigning", High for "the underlying mechanisms are not new"*

## Executive summary

The answer is the same as on 2026-10-08, with more support behind it. Nearly every mechanism in the project is established prior art: hash-linked timestamping (1991), a hash-chained *digital chain of custody* specifically (Bradford & Ray, 2007, newly found), hash-chained forensic audit logs (1999), Merkle history trees with consistency proofs (2009), and witness cosigning of transparency-log checkpoints (2016, with Sigsum matching our threat model). What we can defend as new is narrower: **applying the transparency-log and witness toolkit to a single-custodian forensic chain of custody, as a working non-blockchain system, with an evaluation that states what it does not stop.**

This run queried OpenAlex, which indexes the metadata and abstracts of IEEE, ACM, Elsevier and Springer venues, and the arXiv API. Neither turned up a forensic custody system with independent witnesses or consistency proofs. The closest new work is a preprint posted on 2026-10-18, **"Spectrum of Liability"**, a forensic evidence envelope that detects modification and truncation with hash chains and RFC 3161-timestamped checkpoints, but with no Merkle tree, no consistency proofs and no witnesses. It sharpens the paper's argument rather than undercutting it: a timestamp authority vouches for *when* a checkpoint existed, not that it *extends* the previous one, so it would timestamp a forked history without objection. A witness refuses to. The paper should cite it, along with two forensic items the first scan missed (Horodocs, 2025; Bradford & Ray, 2007).

## 3. What this means for the paper

1. **The central claim stands.** Keep the wording already in v3 ("None of the mechanisms is new… in the sources we could reach we found no prior forensic custody system that combines them").
2. **Add to Related Work, *Tamper-evident logging*:** Bradford & Ray (2007) as the earliest hash-chained *digital chain of custody*.
3. **Add to Related Work, *Custody surveys and evidence containers*:** Horodocs (2025) beside ECo-Bag, as Merkle batching of evidence hashes with an Ethereum anchor.
4. **Add Spectrum of Liability (2026) as the closest forensic work**, with the contrast stated plainly: its checkpoints are timestamped by a TSA, which attests *when* a checkpoint existed but does not check that it extends the previous one, so it would timestamp two different histories of the same log (a later rewrite would at least carry a later time). Our witnesses check consistency before they sign. The two are complementary, which also supports our existing future-work item of an RFC 3161 timestamp on each checkpoint.
5. **Table XI:** add a row for Spectrum of Liability (no ledger: yes; signed actions: TSA tokens, not per-actor; tail cut shown: yes; witnesses: a TSA, no consistency check; own verifier: not stated), and consider putting Horodocs in the ledger row.
6. **Keep the hedge.** The scan is still not a database search with full text.

## Methodology

**Run 2 (2026-10-28).** OpenAlex API, 10 queries, top 15 by relevance each: `"chain of custody" "transparency log"`, `"chain of custody" witness cosign`, `"chain of custody" "consistency proof"`, `"chain of custody" Merkle tree signature`, `"digital evidence" "transparency log"`, `"digital forensics" "signed tree head"`, `Sigsum transparency log`, `"digital evidence" tamper-evident log without blockchain`, `"chain of custody" "hash chain" digital evidence`, `"evidence" "witness cosigning"`. arXiv API, 4 queries, newest first: `all:"chain of custody"`, `all:"transparency log" AND all:witness`, `all:"digital evidence" AND all:Merkle`, `all:forensic AND all:"transparency log"`. Web search: 3 queries (2 extended). Abstracts were pulled from OpenAlex for 11 candidates; 4 pages were fetched (Spectrum of Liability HTML, HANSARD, A2A-CaseVerify, vestige #401) and 1 was blocked (ZAKON). Screening was by one reader (Claude), with no second screener.

**Run 1 (2026-10-08).** 15 web search queries (3 extended), 9 page fetches, and a full read of the 2024 survey. Sub-questions: (1) does witness cosigning or a transparency log appear in forensic custody work; (2) is non-blockchain hash-chained custody already published; (3) do truncation and anchoring appear in the logging literature; (4) are Merkle evidence containers and folder proofs already known; (5) is an independent no-shared-code verifier an established practice. Claims were graded by source type: peer-reviewed and standards, then preprints and design issues, then vendor and practitioner pages.
